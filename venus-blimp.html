Why about 56 km up

Venus's surface is 465°C under 92 bar of CO₂. Climb into the clouds and it cools steadily. Near 55–56 km the air is about 21°C and 0.5 bar, roughly the pressure at the top of Kilimanjaro.

Riding the wind

The upper atmosphere super-rotates. At this altitude the wind runs about 60 m/s westward, so the blimp circles the planet in about a week and the Sun rises in the west.

How it holds altitude

The helium envelope is sealed and rigid-pressure, so its lift depends only on outside air density. To sink, a compressor packs CO₂ from outside into a tank. To rise, it vents some back out.

It has been done

In 1985 the Soviet VEGA 1 and 2 balloons floated near 54 km for about two days each, measuring winds and turbulence on the way.

Air is a lifting gas

Earth air (29 g/mol) is lighter than Venus's CO₂ (43 g/mol). A habitat full of breathable air would float, with about 40% of helium's lift.

Model: temperatures follow an approximate Venus International Reference Atmosphere (VIRA) profile with a hydrostatic pressure column (92.1 bar at the surface). Day/night tides add up to about 2 K near the cloud tops, gravity waves about 1 K, and convective updrafts are strongest near 52 km. Blimp: 5,000 m³ helium envelope, 2,650 kg dry mass, up to 2,500 kg CO₂ ballast, compressor 3 kg/s in, 6 kg/s vent. Vertical motion includes buoyancy, added mass and drag. Horizontally the blimp drifts with the wind. Equatorial track. A simulation for intuition, not mission design.
